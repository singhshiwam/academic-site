About

I am a PhD researcher at the Centre of Excellence in Disaster Mitigation and Management, IIT Roorkee, in the Weather and Climate Research Group led by Dr. Piyush Srivastava. My thesis is on how numerical weather prediction models represent the surface and boundary layer over mountains.

Most of my days go between two kinds of data. One is high-frequency turbulence measurements from a flux tower in the Central Himalaya. The other is output from the WRF model, which I run at kilometre resolution over Uttarakhand and the wider Northwest Himalaya. I use the tower data to check how well the model computes the exchange of heat and momentum at the ground over steep terrain, and to find out why it goes wrong when it does.

I came to atmospheric science from pure mathematics. My master's projects at IIT Hyderabad were on the Riemann-Roch theorem for function fields and on arcs in finite projective spaces. That training helps in modelling. A parameterization scheme is a set of differential equations with closure assumptions, and I prefer to read and check those myself instead of only changing model settings.

Alongside the thesis I contribute the atmospheric modelling to group studies of extreme rainfall, landslides and urban heat stress, including the July 2024 Wayanad landslide.
